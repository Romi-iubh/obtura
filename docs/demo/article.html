A year in the allotment

Planning the spring beds

Start with a plan on paper: which beds get sun all day, which stay damp, and where last year's tomatoes grew. Rotate the families so the soil rests.

Club tip 1: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Soil first

A thin layer of compost each autumn does more than any fertiliser. Leave the worms to dig it in over the winter.

Club tip 2: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Sowing indoors

Peppers and aubergines need a long season: sow them in March on a warm windowsill and keep the compost just moist.

Club tip 3: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Hardening off

Two weeks before planting out, put the seedlings outside by day and bring them in at night. Wind makes the stems strong.

Club tip 4: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Watering

Water deeply twice a week rather than a little every day: roots follow the water down and the plants cope better with dry spells.

Club tip 5: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Companion planting

Marigolds near the tomatoes, nasturtiums along the beans, basil wherever there is room. The bees will thank you.

Club tip 6: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Slugs and snails

Go out with a torch after rain. A ring of grit around the young lettuces helps; beer traps work too.

Club tip 7: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Harvest and share

Pick courgettes small and often. Bring what you cannot eat to the Saturday seed swap table by the gate.

Club tip 8: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Saving seeds

Let a few of the best plants go to seed. Dry the seeds in paper bags, label them, and keep them cool and dark.

Club tip 9: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.

Winter tasks

Clean and oil the tools, mend the fences, and order seeds early: the popular varieties go fast.

Club tip 10: ask at the hut on Saturday mornings.
